# ClearSky v2.0: assessment and design changes

v2.0 re-centres the app on one job: **will the air be clear enough for sharp, clean distant landscape shots, and when
and which way should I shoot?** Moon, aurora and the other shoot types stay as small extras. This note records the
assessment of v1.1, what working photographers do, the decisions that follow, and where they depart from
docs/design.md (D1 to D22 are untouched; the departures are additions inside D9, D15 and D16).

## Assessment of v1.1

**What it did well.** A transparent hourly score with reasons, a sound factor set for haze (dew point, particulates,
visibility, mixing height, air-mass change, rain scrub), honest handling of missing data, confidence by lead time, an
NWS cross-check, climate percentile for dew point, and a self-updating PWA.

**Where it fell short for a distant-landscape photographer.**

1. **It judged the air at your feet, not between you and the subject.** A smoke plume or a damp valley 30 miles away
   decides the shot. Nothing let you say how far or which way you shoot.
2. **Subject distance was ignored.** A ridge 5 miles away shrugs off haze that ruins a range 50 miles away.
3. **No sun-versus-view logic.** Looking into a low sun adds glare and forward-scattered haze; side-on is where a
   polarizer works best. Photographers choose direction as much as time.
4. **Telephoto sharpness was treated as haze only.** Heat shimmer (mid-day, warm, sunny) and low cloud hiding ridges
   were absent from Clarity.
5. **Confidence ignored forecast spread.** Dew point is the biggest clarity driver, and whether the models agree on it
   says more about a day-5 score than lead time alone (D16 already named forecast spread).
6. **No look-ahead.** The week was a strip of cards. Pros plan around the air mass: when a front clears the haze,
   when moisture returns, and which day to hold out for.
7. **Hard to read on a phone.** Dense desktop-first layout, tiny tap targets, no way to see which layer (moisture,
   smoke, cloud) spoils a given hour, and no way to turn a good window into a reminder.

## What photographers do (research, 2026-11-05)

Sources were forums, pro blogs and magazines found by search; several fetches timed out, so these are practitioner
habits, not measurements. Where a claim is a rule of thumb, the app treats it as a weighted factor, not a threshold.

- **Layers, not totals.** Cloud percentage alone is not enough; photographers read low, mid and high layers
  (Windy, Clear Outside, Photo WX style grids) and cross-check models, treating the result as odds. Hence the Layers
  tab and the ensemble spread.
- **Dryness and air-mass change.** Dew point and the temperature-dew point gap are the staple signals for haze and
  fog; the day after a cold front is the clear-air day. (Already in the model; v2 surfaces it as an outlook.)
- **Smoke and aerosols** are checked separately because they defeat otherwise dry, clear air.
- **Direction and light.** Pros pick direction relative to the sun, work away from or at right angles to it, and
  reach for a polarizer at about 90 degrees to the sun.
- **Timing against the day's heat.** Long lenses are best early or late, before and after ground heating drives
  shimmer. Wind over a moderate speed shakes tripods.
- **Plan for odds and a plan B.** Forecasts are hedged with several sources; reminders the evening before are normal.

## Design decisions in v2.0

| ID | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V1 | Clarity is the default and primary profile; the other five are secondary "looks" | Matches the main use. Golden, Fog, Reflections, Soft and Wildlife stay available, unchanged. |
| V2 | Subject distance (Near / Mid / Far) multiplies the Clarity weights (`DISTANCE` in core.js); Mid is the plain profile | Haze compounds with distance; shimmer scales with focal length. Weights are renormalised to 100. |
| V3 | View direction (8 compass points or Any) adds a `viewLight` factor, 5% of Clarity, only when set | Looking into a low sun is the worst case; side-on is best; overhead sun has no directional effect. |
| V4 | Line of sight: two points at 50% and 100% of the subject distance along the bearing, one weather and one air-quality call each, merged by `mergePath` | Dew point and humidity average; visibility, particulates and low cloud take the worst point. Clarity only. Fails soft to your spot. |
| V5 | Clarity gains `heatShimmer` (6), `lowCloudClear` (3) and `viewLight` (5); dew point 30 to 26, particulates 18 to 17, ventilation 8 to 6, air-mass 10 to 8, rain scrub 6 to 5, wind 8 to 6, humidity 4 to 6, sky cover 6 to 2 | Telephoto sharpness is more than haze. Sums to 100. Golden scenarios 1 to 5 still pass unchanged. |
| V6 | Confidence also drops one step when the 10th to 90th percentile of ensemble dew point spans 10 F or more; shown as a range ("Dew point 34 to 41 F across forecast models") | Implements the "forecast spread" input D16 already named, from Open-Meteo's GFS ensemble (31 members, 29 KB, about 0.7 s). Never raises confidence. |
| V7 | Air-mass outlook: daily daylight-mean dew point; a change of 8 F or more between days is a "drying" or "moistening" event, shown as plain sentences and a week chart | Answers "which day do I hold out for". |
| V8 | Mobile-first shell: four tabs (Plan, Day, Layers, More), thumb-height controls, week as a list, fixed bottom tab bar that becomes a top bar on desktop | The app is used on a phone before a drive. |
| V9 | Layers tab: hour-by-hour grid of high, mid and low cloud, dew point, humidity, visibility, PM2.5, aerosol, mixing height, wind, rain, temperature, plus the line-of-sight rows when a direction is set; cells tinted by how good that layer is | Shows which layer is spoiling an hour, the way pros read forecast grids. |
| V10 | Field notes: plain advice from the factors (point away from the sun, polarizer direction, humid or smoky warnings, shimmer timing, wind, rising or building moisture, haze pooling) | A score without an action is half the value (design principle in section 2 of the first tab). |
| V11 | "Add reminder" builds an .ics file locally with an alarm one hour before the window | Turns a window into an alarm without accounts or push. |
| V12 | Small extras: moon phase and illumination on the Day tab; NOAA SWPC Kp forecast gives an "aurora watch" line only when it reaches your geomagnetic latitude | Cheap, optional, not part of any score. |
| V13 | `past_days` stays 2; no new thresholds in the UI (distance and direction are named choices; all curves and multipliers live in core.js) | Keeps the rule in CLAUDE.md. |

## Not built (and why)

- **Light-pollution (Bortle) data, a Night sky profile, rainbow and storm-clearing detection.** Outside the main
  focus; Night sky needs a dataset that has not been chosen (see design.md, Scoring and profiles).
- **Terrain.** The line of sight is two weather points, not a terrain model. The app cannot tell that a ridge blocks
  the path or that a valley pools haze; field notes say so where relevant.
- **Auto-fit of weights from the shot log** (design milestone M5) is still planned.
